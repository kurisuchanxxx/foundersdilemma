# Student manual

## The goal

Found a startup and run it for about four years. At the end, what counts is **how much the founders' shares are worth** (unless your instructor chooses a different criterion): a company can be worth a lot while its founders own little of it, after funding rounds, option pools and liquidation preferences. Every round you will have to balance growth, survival and control.

Your grade, however, does not depend mainly on your ranking: what counts is the quality of your decisions and your ability to explain them (see [Assessment and rubrics](05-assessment-and-rubrics-en.md)).

## Installation and access

- Download the app for macOS or Windows from your instructor's link, or open `FoundersDilemma-web.html` in your browser. The game also runs online at [kurisuchanxxx.github.io/foundersdilemma/play](https://kurisuchanxxx.github.io/foundersdilemma/play/).
- **Classroom mode**: *Join a class (team)* > *Open the game file* > choose your team.
- **Hot-seat mode**: your instructor calls you to the computer; press *We are ...: show our company*. The other teams look away.

## The screens

| Screen | What you find |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Cash, runway, monthly revenue, customers, PMF, valuation, charts and the mentor's comments |
| **Decisions** | The round's choices, split into tabs, with the projection updated in real time |
| **Team & hiring** | Founders, employees and the results of the latest job offers (candidates and offers are in **Decisions > Founders & team**) |
| **Funding** | Cap table, investors and their requirements, funding history, outcome of the latest pitches (pitches, grants, loans, term sheets and acquisition offers are handled in **Decisions > Finance & exit**) |
| **Product & PMF** | MVP progress, Sean Ellis test, technical debt and bugs |
| **Market** | Market size and the part you can reach with your strategy, market adoption and the adoption curve, unit economics, the teams and the external competitors with their latest moves |
| **News & reputation** | Market events and the company's reputation |
| **Leaderboard** | Founder wealth, valuation, equity and control (the crown shows who also controls the board) |
| **Round reports** | What happened in each round |
| **Learn** | How to play and 61 glossary cards with formulas and references. Every *?* icon in the app opens the card for that concept |

## Each round's decisions

| Tab | Decisions | What to watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Budget & price** | Monthly spending on R&D, marketing and operations; pricing model (subscription, freemium, usage-based); price; sales channel | Burn determines runway. A price far above willingness to pay slows sales; a price too low burns margin. Freemium lowers CAC but also willingness to pay and margin |
| **Product** | Target customers (niche, broad market, premium); customer discovery (none, light, deep); development approach (rush, balanced, quality first); launch; managing technical debt | A niche makes you learn faster but is small: when you saturate it, widen your target. Changing target customers after launch costs customers. Discovery raises PMF but costs time and money. Rushed development builds up technical debt and bugs |
| **Founders & team** | Founders' weekly hours; vacation; team offsite; offers to candidates (salary and equity) and answers to their counter-offers; layoffs | 80-hour weeks increase output but also stress: burnout is expensive |
| **Reputation** | No action, PR campaign, conferences and events, community building | Cheap and slow to grow, but it helps customer acquisition, hiring and fundraising |
| **Finance & exit** | Pitches to investors, grants, loans, crowdfunding; accepting or rejecting term sheets and acquisition offers (also from other teams) | Every euro you raise has a price: equity, board seats, interest, liquidation preferences. Investors fund few companies per round: if other teams pitch the same investor, the strongest one wins |
| **Deals** | Propose or accept partnerships; make a cash offer to acquire another company | The answer arrives in the following round. A partnership lasts three rounds and helps both sides; an acquisition is paid with your cash |
| **Founder network** | Up to two actions towards other teams: shout-outs, mentorship, taunts, attempts to poach talent | Everything is public and ends up in the news; relationships between teams remember what happened |
| **Dilemma** (when there is one) | A hard choice: co-founders in conflict, a big customer asking for customizations, dark patterns, a pivot... | There is no right answer: every option has consequences, some of them delayed |
| **Review & submit** | Final check and warnings | In Classroom mode the button locks your decisions and exports the file to hand in to your instructor |

Before submitting, look at the **projection** (cash at the end of the round, runway, expected customers): it is an estimate, because the market and the other teams' moves are not known yet.

## Concepts to keep an eye on

- **Runway** = cash / monthly net burn. Below 6 months, start looking for money: a round takes time.
- **Product-market fit**: the game measures a score from 0 to 100 and the share of users who would be *very disappointed* without the product. PMF is reached at 60 points, which corresponds to 40% in the Sean Ellis test.
- **Chasm**: market adoption grows during the game and usually, around the middle, moves past the early adopters (16%). From then on you are selling to the early majority, who are pragmatic: with a PMF below 55 they still buy, but winning them costs much more (CAC +50%, +20% if you started from a niche). The mentor warns you when the chasm is approaching.
- **LTV/CAC**: at least 3 is healthy; below 1, every new customer loses you money.
- **Shared market**: teams in the same sector share the same potential customers, and the external competitors react (price wars, campaigns, new versions): their moves appear in the news.
- **Beachhead**: starting from a niche helps you find PMF sooner, but the market you can reach is about a third of the sector.
- **Dilution**: every funding round reduces your stake. An option pool created before the round (the *option pool shuffle*) weighs on the existing shareholders (you, the employees, earlier investors, SAFEs that convert), not on the new investor. SAFEs that have not converted yet are already counted in your stake, at their caps.
- **Liquidation preference**: in an exit, each investor chooses between getting its capital back first (1x, or 2x in rescue rounds) and converting to common shares; what is left goes to all holders of common shares (founders, employees and investors who converted) in proportion to their stakes.
- **Rich or King**: keeping the majority of the votes and of the board means controlling the company, but often growing more slowly. Few teams manage both.

## The first round, step by step

At your first game a two-minute **guided tour** starts and shows you where everything is; you can replay it from the **Learn** page.

1. Read the **Dashboard** and your sector's card in **Market**.
2. In **Decisions > Product** choose your target customers (before launch you can change them for free) and at least light discovery: in the first rounds, learning costs less than building the wrong thing.
3. In **Budget & price** keep your burn compatible with at least 9-12 months of runway.
4. Consider a **grant** or an **accelerator** in **Finance & exit**: non-dilutive or barely dilutive money is precious at the start.
5. Answer the **dilemma**, if there is one, and write down in your decision memo right away why you chose as you did.
6. **Review & submit**: read the warnings, then submit (in Classroom mode: hand the file in to your instructor). **Never skip submitting**: a team that does not decide runs on autopilot while the market moves, and loses development and sales capacity, morale and investor confidence, more with every round it skips. If you don't want to change anything, submit the decisions as they are.

## Tips

- Write down your hypotheses before deciding and check them in the round report: that is the *build-measure-learn* logic.
- Don't chase valuation at all costs: the default leaderboard measures the wealth of the **founders**.
- For your grade, a well-analysed failure is worth more than a lucky win.
- Use the **Learn** cards: they cover the same concepts you will use in the exam and in your own project.
