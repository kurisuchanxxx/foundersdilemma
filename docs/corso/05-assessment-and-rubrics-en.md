# Assessment and rubrics

## Weights

| Component | Weight | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision memos | 30% | Team | After each round (the best 6 out of 10 are graded, so one weak memo does not weigh too much) |
| Final report and post-mortem | 30% | Team | One week after the last session |
| Final presentation | 20% | Team | Last session |
| In-game result | 10% | Team | End of the game |
| Peer evaluation and participation | 10% | Individual | End of the module |

The individual grade is the team grade adjusted by the peer evaluation (for example with a factor from 0.8 to 1.1).

---

## 1. Decision memo (one page per round)

### Template

```
Team: ............................   Round: ....   Period: ...........

1. Situation (3 lines)
   Cash, runway, PMF, customers, LTV/CAC. What changed since the previous round, and why.

2. Hypotheses
   What do we expect to happen with this round's decisions? (e.g. with deep discovery
   PMF passes 40 within two rounds; at a price of €49 churn stays below 5%)

3. Key decisions (max 3) and rationale
   Link each decision to a course concept.

4. Risks and plan B
   What could go wrong and how we will react.

5. Check on the previous round
   Did the hypotheses in the previous memo come true? What did we learn?
```

### Rubric (score from 0 to 10)

| Criterion | 0-1 Insufficient | 2 Sufficient | 3 Good | 4 Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading the data (×1) | Numbers missing or copied without comment | Correct numbers, little interpretation | Correct interpretation of the main indicators | Identifies causes and weak signals |
| Hypotheses and decisions (×1.5) | Decisions not justified | Generic reasons | Testable hypotheses, linked to theory | Testable hypotheses, alternatives considered, explicit trade-offs |
| Learning (×0.5) | No check on the previous round | Superficial check | Honest comparison of expectations and outcomes | Revises the mental model and the strategy accordingly |

Score = (sum of the weighted points) / 12 × 10.

## 2. Final report and post-mortem (max 8 pages)

### Required structure

Start from the **Team report** the game generates (button in *Final results*): it already contains the numbers, charts and round-by-round decisions, so the report can focus on analysis.

1. **Summary** (half a page): the company's story in five lines and the final result.
2. **Strategy**: sector, founding team, business model, and how they changed.
3. **Three key decisions**: context, alternatives, choice, outcome, what you would do differently. Use the data in the round reports and, if your instructor provides them, the CSV files.
4. **Product-market fit and growth**: when and how you reached it (or why you did not); unit economics over time.
5. **Funding and control**: initial and final cap table, dilution, preferences, board control. Where you place yourselves on the *Rich or King?* chart, and why.
6. **Dilemmas and ethics**: at least one dilemma you faced, with its consequences.
7. **Reality check**: a real case similar to your trajectory; where the game simplifies.
8. **Lessons learned**: five lessons you can transfer to a real project.

### Rubric (score from 0 to 30)

| Criterion | Weight | Excellent | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data-driven analysis | 25% | Indicators used to explain causes, relevant charts | Data present but only descriptive | Claims without data |
| Use of theory | 25% | Course concepts applied precisely and critically | Concepts cited correctly | Theory missing or wrong |
| Quality of the post-mortem | 20% | Recognizes the team's own mistakes, biases and luck; credible alternatives | Recognizes some mistakes | Blames everything on chance or on others |
| Finance and control | 15% | Cap table and waterfall explained correctly; the trade-off between wealth and control argued | Partial explanation | Calculation or conceptual errors |
| Clarity and form | 15% | Concise, well structured, sources cited | Understandable | Disorganized or wordy |

## 3. Final presentation (8 minutes plus 4 for questions)

| Criterion | Weight | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Story and message | 30% | A clear thread, a thesis on what worked and what did not |
| Evidence | 30% | Two or three well-chosen charts or numbers |
| Reflection | 25% | Credible lessons learned, linked to theory |
| Handling questions | 15% | To-the-point answers, admits limits |

---

## 4. In-game result (in bands)

To avoid rewarding luck or score-chasing, the result weighs 10% and is graded in bands, based on the leaderboard criterion chosen at the start (recommended: founder wealth).

| Outcome | Points out of 10 |
|---|---|
| Top third of the leaderboard | 10 |
| Middle third | 8 |
| Bottom third, company still active or sold | 7 |
| Bankruptcy with a convincing post-mortem | 6 |
| Bankruptcy without a convincing post-mortem | 4 |

Optional bonus (+1, without going above 10): achievements unlocked and choices consistent with the declared strategy (for example PMF reached before scaling marketing, visible in the history in the team report).

---

## 5. Peer evaluation

An anonymous form at the end of the module: each student distributes 100 points among the other members of their team (excluding themselves) and answers two open questions (*who contributed most to the analysis?*, *what could the team have done better?*). The individual factor is the average received divided by the team average, capped between 0.8 and 1.1.

---

## Integrity

- Decisions belong to the team: the use of artificial intelligence tools to write memos and reports follows the course rules and must be declared.
- Editing game or decision files by hand, and opening the game file to try decisions in advance, are not allowed. The game only checks that the files are intact and for the right round, not who prepared them: the instructor keeps the official copy and the files received (see the [Instructor guide](03-instructor-guide-en.md), section 7).
