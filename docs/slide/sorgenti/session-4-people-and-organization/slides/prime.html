The first hires

The first people make the company.

Generalists first

In the search phase you need people who do many things and change roles when the plan changes.

Right for the stage

Someone perfect for a 500-person company can be wrong for a company of five.

Mistakes are expensive

A bad hire costs more than a salary: founder time, morale, culture.

“Hire for strength rather than lack of weakness.” Ben Horowitz

In the game. New hires take two months to ramp up, and juniors work at 85% if they have no mentor.

Session 4 · People and organization

03

In the early years every person carries enormous weight: they bring skills, but also habits that become the company’s culture. In the search phase you need generalists who can change roles; specialists become valuable once the model is found and has to scale. A bad hire does not cost just the salary: it costs the founders’ time to onboard the person and later to manage the exit, and it weighs on everyone else’s morale. The line comes from Ben Horowitz, The Hard Thing About Hard Things (2014), who credits it to advice from Colin Powell: better someone with an exceptional strength you need right now, flaws and all, than someone with no flaws and no strengths. Question for the class: which role would you hire first in your company, and why that one?
