John Pencavel (Stanford, IZA Discussion Paper 8129, 2014, later published in the Economic Journal) analyzed data on workers, mostly women, in British munitions factories during World War I. Up to about 49 hours a week, output grew in proportion to hours; beyond that it grew less and less, and at 70 hours it was close to what 56 hours produced. Missing a weekly day of rest also hurt: six eight-hour days produced a little more than 70 hours spread over seven days. It is an observational study of manual work, so treat it as an indication, but the lesson for founders holds: past a certain point, extra hours are paid for in mistakes and fatigue. Question for the class: how many hours a week do you think you could sustain for a whole year, not just a month?
