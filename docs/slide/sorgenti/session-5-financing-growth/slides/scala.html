Sources of capital

Every euro you raise has a price, even when you can’t see it.

SourceWhat it costs, besides money
Revenue and savings (bootstrapping)Slower growth, personal risk for the founders
Friends and familyRelationships at stake, expectations often left unwritten
Grants and subsidized loansTime, restrictions on use, reporting; no equity given up
Business angelsEquity, and an experienced partner who will want a say
Venture capitalEquity, board seats, and very fast growth to deliver

In the game. Grants, loans, crowdfunding, angels, an accelerator and venture capital each have different requirements, and investors have limited slots and capital for the whole class.

Session 5 · Financing growth

03

The sources roughly follow the life of the company: first the founders’ own money and customers’ money, then friends and family, grants and subsidized loans, business angels, and finally venture capital (seed, Series A and later rounds). The point of the lesson is that no source is free: debt has to be repaid, grants take time and come with strings, equity costs ownership and often control, and venture capital comes with an expectation of very fast growth. Question for the class: for your startup, which price are you most willing to pay, and which one not at all?
