The figures come from the same Wasserman article (Harvard Business Review, February 2008), based on 212 American startups: three years after founding, half of the founders were no longer CEO, and fewer than a quarter still led the company at its IPO. The replacement often comes right after a success, when the company needs different skills and the board, where investors carry more and more weight, makes the call. Jobs was pushed aside in 1985 and returned to Apple in 1997; Dorsey gave up the Twitter CEO role in October 2008 and took it back in 2015. Question for the class: at what point should the founder be the one to propose a new CEO?
