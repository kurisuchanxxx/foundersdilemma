Pricing models

How to get paid.

Subscription

A fixed fee every month or year: recurring, predictable revenue.

Netflix, Salesforce

Freemium

A free tier that brings in users; few of them upgrade to paid. A good rate is 3-5%.

Dropbox, Spotify

Usage-based

Customers pay for what they use: revenue grows along with the customer.

Amazon Web Services, Twilio

In the game. Freemium lowers CAC and multiplies word of mouth, but cuts willingness to pay and margin; usage-based pricing makes customers less price-sensitive.

Session 3 · Go-to-market and unit economics

05

Each model moves the numbers. Subscription makes revenue predictable and turns churn into the main problem. Freemium uses the free product as an acquisition channel: it only works if free users are cheap to serve and some of them convert to paid. For self-serve products a conversion rate of 3-5% is considered good and 6-8% great (Lenny Rachitsky, analysis of more than a thousand products; a fifth of products stay below 2.5%). Usage-based pricing ties cost to the value received and grows with the customer, but makes revenue less predictable. Question for the class: which model best tracks the value your customer receives?
