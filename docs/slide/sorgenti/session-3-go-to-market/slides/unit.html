Unit economics

Three numbers that tell you if a customer pays off.

CAC

acquisition spend ÷ new customers

What it costs to win a customer: marketing and sales, salespeople included.

LTV

price × gross margin × lifetime

The margin a customer leaves over their whole lifetime; average lifetime is 1 ÷ churn.

Payback

CAC ÷ (price × gross margin)

The months it takes to earn back what you spent to acquire them.

David Skok’s rules of thumb: LTV at least 3 times CAC, and CAC recovered in under 12 months.

Session 3 · Go-to-market and unit economics

06

Unit economics looks at one customer at a time: if each customer costs more than it brings in, growing loses more money, not less. The two thresholds come from David Skok (Matrix Partners, SaaS Metrics 2.0): they are rules of thumb for subscription software, not laws. An LTV/CAC far above 3 can mean the company is underinvesting in growth; a long payback requires a lot of cash even when LTV is good. Watch the CAC: it has to include every acquisition cost, salespeople’s salaries included. The next slide runs the numbers on an example.
