The pivot

Change the strategy, keep the vision.

2005

YouTube

It starts as a video dating site. Nobody uploads videos to introduce themselves, so the founders open it up to any video.

2006

Twitter

It is born inside Odeo, a podcasting platform overtaken by iTunes. An internal short-message project becomes the company.

2010

Instagram

Burbn is a check-in app with too many features. Users mostly share photos, so the founders keep only that.

In the game. In the “Pivot or persevere?” dilemma, a pivot brings an idea with new potential, but it costs customers and brand.

Session 2 · From problem to MVP

09

For Eric Ries a pivot is a structured course correction, designed to test a new fundamental hypothesis about the product, the strategy or the engine of growth. It is not starting over: you keep what you have learned. YouTube (2005): according to co-founder Steve Chen, it was meant as a video dating site; nobody uploaded videos, so they opened it to everyone. Twitter (2006): it began as an internal project at Odeo, a podcasting startup put in trouble when Apple added podcasts to iTunes. Instagram (2010): Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger stripped down Burbn, a check-in app, keeping only the feature users actually used, photos. Question for the class: what signal in the data would make you decide to pivot, and what would tell you to persevere?
