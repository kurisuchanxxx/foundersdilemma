Lesson

Eric Ries, The Lean Startup (2011)

A business plan is a list of hypotheses.

Ries calls them “leap-of-faith assumptions”: the assumptions everything else depends on. There are two, and they are the first to test.

Value hypothesis

Does the product really create value for the people who use it? You measure it by what customers do, not by what they say.

Growth hypothesis

How will new customers discover the product? Word of mouth, advertising, direct sales: each one has a different cost.

In the game. The decision memo asks for each round’s hypotheses and, in the next round, the check: did they come true?

Eric Ries (The Lean Startup, 2011) calls the hypotheses the whole plan rests on “leap-of-faith assumptions”: the value hypothesis (the product creates value for the people who use it) and the growth hypothesis (how new customers will discover it). The practical point is that a business plan is not something to execute but something to test, starting from the riskiest hypothesis. Tests count when they measure behavior (sign-ups, purchases, return visits) rather than opinions (“yes, I’d use it”). Question for the class: what is your company’s riskiest hypothesis, and which of the two kinds is it?
