Forms of exit

The exit is the moment shares turn into money.

IPO

The company lists on the stock market and stays independent. Rare, slow and expensive.

Strategic

A company in the same industry buys the product, the customers or the technology.

Financial

A private equity fund buys the cash flows of a company that is already profitable.

Acqui-hire

The buyer wants the team; the product often shuts down.

The vast majority of venture-backed startups exit through an acquisition, not an IPO.

In the game. Offers come from a corporate VC that already holds shares, from a private equity fund if the company is profitable, or from a strategic acquirer; the acqui-hire is one of the dilemmas.

Session 6 · Exit and final debriefing

03

For founders, employees and investors, shares are wealth only on paper until there is an exit. The main forms: the initial public offering (IPO), which keeps the company independent and makes its shares tradable on the market; an acquisition by a strategic buyer, who pays more when it sees synergies with its own business; an acquisition by a financial buyer, typically a private equity fund, which looks at cash flows; and the acqui-hire, where the buyer is mostly after the team. There are also secondary sales, in which a shareholder sells their shares to another investor without the company changing hands. PitchBook-NVCA data have shown for years that acquisitions far outnumber IPOs by count, while IPOs carry a lot of weight by value. Question for the class: which form of exit would you prefer for your company, and who would be its natural buyer?
