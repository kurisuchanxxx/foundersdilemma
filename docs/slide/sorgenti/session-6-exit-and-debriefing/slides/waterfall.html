Lesson

The waterfall

The sale price is not what the founders take home.

Example: founders at 60%, one investor at 40% with €8M invested and a 1x non-participating preference

Sale price

€12M

The number that makes the headlines.

To the investor

€8M

The preference is paid first: 40% would be worth only €4.8M.

To the founders

€4M

33% of the price, with 60% of the shares.

In a €40M sale the investor converts and takes its 40% (€16M), and the founders get €24M, or 60%.

In the game. Accepting an offer splits the price through the waterfall: 1x preferences, or 2x for rescue rounds, are paid first; the unallocated pool gets nothing.

Session 6 · Exit and final debriefing

05

The waterfall is the order in which the sale price flows down to the shareholders. With a 1x non-participating preference, the investor takes the better of two options: getting back what it invested, or converting to common stock and taking its share. In the example (numbers made up for the lesson), at €12M the 40% would be worth €4.8M, less than the €8M invested, so the investor takes the preference and the founders are left with €4M: a third of the price, despite owning 60% of the shares. At €40M converting pays more, and the split goes back to pro rata. Preferences matter a lot in low-priced sales and barely at all in high-priced ones. This builds on the session 5 lesson on term sheet clauses. Question for the class: on your cap table, below what price would the founders get nothing?
