Lesson

When to sell

Five questions to ask about an offer.

01

Is the price higher than what the company will be worth tomorrow, allowing for the risk of never getting there?

02

How much actually reaches the founders and employees, after preferences?

03

Who decides: do the founders still control the board?

04

What happens to the team, the customers and the product after the sale?

05

Is it the right moment: the market, the investment climate, the cash you have left?

In the game. Offers range from 0.9 to about 2 times the valuation, depending on the buyer; accepting one is an exit, and the price goes to the shareholders through the waterfall.

Session 6 · Exit and final debriefing

06

Selling is a choice between a certain price today and an uncertain value tomorrow. The first question is the heart of the Facebook and Yahoo case: saying no makes sense only if you believe, with good reasons, that the company will be worth much more and that you will actually get there. The second points back to the waterfall: the same price can be great for the investors and disappointing for the founders. The third is a reminder that if the founders have lost control of the board, the decision may not be theirs to make. The fourth is about people: employees with options, customers, a product the buyer might shut down. The fifth is about context: acquisition prices rise and fall with the market. Question for the class: if you got an offer today, which of these questions would make you say no?
