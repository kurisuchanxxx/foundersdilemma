Lesson

Three cases

Two sales, and one offer turned down.

2012

Instagram

Facebook buys it for about $1 billion in cash and stock, eighteen months after launch. It has 13 employees.

2014

WhatsApp

Facebook buys it for about $19 billion: $16 billion in cash and stock, $3 billion in restricted stock for those who stay.

2006

Facebook

Yahoo offers $1 billion. Zuckerberg says no: he is convinced the company is worth far more on its own.

In the game. Once you sell you are no longer King: on the Rich or King chart, the exit turns control into cashed-in wealth.

Session 6 · Exit and final debriefing

04

Instagram: announced on April 9, 2012, for about $1 billion in cash and Facebook stock; according to TechCrunch the company had 13 employees, and the app had launched in October 2010. WhatsApp: a February 2014 deal worth about $16 billion, $4 billion in cash and $12 billion in stock, plus $3 billion in restricted stock units for the founders and employees, vesting over four years: an example of how a buyer uses vesting to keep the team. Facebook: in 2006 Yahoo offered $1 billion; Zuckerberg turned it down, convinced the company could grow much bigger. Sources: Facebook’s announcement (April 2012) and press coverage at the time; the SEC filing for the WhatsApp deal (February 2014); accounts by Peter Thiel and in the Facebook biographies. Question for the class: hindsight makes it easy; with what information would you have decided, back then, to sell or to say no?
